# Teamwork

Throughout the project, we have primarily coordinated our work through GitHub, using issues to distribute tasks and feature branches and pull requests to manage contributions. This allowed us to work on different parts of the application in parallel while keeping changes relatively isolated.

With every release, we became more deliberate about how we organized this work. For example, we introduced a `CONTRIBUTING.md` file describing our development practices, as well as issue and pull request templates to encourage more consistent issues and PRs. We also started making greater and more deliberate use of pull request reviews, so we could communicate and assign responsibility more effectively. These practices helped establish a more predictable workflow and made it easier to understand what individual contributions were intended to accomplish.

However, we learned that introducing tools and guidelines does not automatically ensure that they are used consistently. Some issues lacked sufficient detail, and contribution guidelines were not always followed. This occasionally made it harder to understand the scope of a task or review a contribution. We also experienced situations where several changes had to be integrated and reviewed at once.

We had weekly team meetings where we worked together, along with good communication within the team outside of those times. 
While the whole group worked together in the team meetings, with discussions and collaboration, we also met other than the regular meetings, and sometimes in smaller groups.
We are also all from the same study programme, which made it easy for us to work out small changes and meet without arranging an entire meeting. 
It was however hard to find enough time to work together outside of the team meetings where all members were available, as we have quite different schedules, and different preferences in how we like to work.
